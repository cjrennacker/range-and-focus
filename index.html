About the brand

Where the trail meets the wall.

Range & Focus is a luxury fine art photography brand. The work is landscape-driven, cinematic, and place-based; built for people who want more than decoration on their walls.

Every image is earned on foot, often miles from the nearest road, in glowing light that only lasts about thirty seconds. The photographs are limited edition, every edition is intentional, and the experience of owning one should feel like you were there too.

Right now, the site works. But it's lost the focus (pun intended) by focusing on scents and apparel. Your job is to close that gap... and then some.

The audience

Our customers are "seekers."

"They look for unique, one-of-a-kind items, and don't want mass-produced decor. They want a piece from a trail they've been on before, that becomes a story they can hang on their wall."

Range & Focus customers are outdoor enthusiasts, collectors, and hikers who value the story behind the art. They are driven by experience and authenticity. They buy slowly and deliberately. When they invest in a piece, it means they have a personal connection to it.

They might encounter the brand by meeting the artist on a trail in a national park, on Instagram during a late-night scroll, or through a friend who already owns a print. However they arrive, the brand needs to feel immediately credible, and worth their time.

The Collector
Values scarcity, provenance, and craft. Researches before buying. Wants to feel they discovered something no one else has.
The Hiker
Discovered the brand while on trail. Made a personall connection to the artist. Responds to stories and places they've been.
The Gifter
Buying for someone who just bought a home, moved, or remodeled. Needs confidence the piece will be special and arrive safely.
The Decorator
Needs the ability to project the artwork on a client's wall in real time to get the client to understand the vision and commit.
About the artist

The miles behind the work.

More than thirteen years ago, Cameron Rennacker picked up a camera for the first time and fell in love. Every day, he would hike along the lakes, creeks, and forests of California's San Bernardino Mountains, where he grew up, photographing water flowing over rocks, and sweeping views of the Inland Empire from the cliffside highway that ran through town.

Photography became his sport. He studied the masters, practiced obsessively, and returned to the same locations over and over to test new compositions, exposure times, and lighting conditions until he got it just right.

He met his husband Josh in 2016, and from the beginning they bonded over a shared spirit of adventure to get in the car and drive for hours until they found somewhere new, never taking the same road twice. That spirit has carried them to 3 countries, 26 states, 38 national parks, and more than 300,000 miles of road.

Follow the journey on Instagram @rangeandfocus. And if you have questions about national parks, hike recommendations, or where to go for a new adventure, send a message. By this point, they're fairly qualified to answer.

Scope of work

Your challenge.

Each team should choose three items they feel most capable of producing from the list below. Range & Focus prides itself in not using AI to generate any work for its brand. We want to be sure to retain that hand-crafted quality because it is something our customers value.

01Web & UX

A complete high-fidelity website redesign built with Shopify as the destination. Output stops at high-fidelity. You are not expected to build it, but every decision should be made with Shopify in mind. The experience must prioritize storytelling over simple "add to cart" mechanics. This is a gallery that happens to sell things, not a store that happens to look nice.

  • Full information architecture and site map
  • User flows for all key tasks (browse, bid, buy, AR preview)
  • Low-fidelity wireframes in Figma
  • High-fidelity prototypes for mobile and desktop
  • AR room visualization feature
  • Collector's vault auction interface
  • Gallery edition browsing and purchase flow
  • Post-purchase flow including size/frame selection and CoA delivery
  • Usability testing with at least 10 participants
  • Developer-ready design documentation
The site should feel like the landscapes: immersive, unhurried, and impossible to rush through. If someone is clicking just to check out, you've designed the wrong thing. Make them savor it, but don't make things impossible to buy.
02AR room viewer

Buyers should be able to see a Range & Focus print hanging on their actual wall, including the chosen frame, the actual image, and approximately accurate sizing, before they bid or buy. This feature must be mobile-friendly and should feel like a natural part of the purchase experience, not a tech demo bolted on afterward.

  • Research and recommend an AR solution (Shopify native .usdz/.glb, Zakeke, Threekit, or other)
  • Design the UX flow for triggering and using the feature
  • Account for all print sizes and frame options
  • Must work on iPhone — this is where most customers will be
  • Propose how the feature is introduced to new users
The best version of this feature doesn't make people feel like they're using technology. It makes them feel like they're standing in their living room with a print in their hands.
03Collector's vault

Range & Focus sells special collector's editions in runs of five. Each month, a selection goes up for auction. When an edition sells, it is gone — possibly for years, possibly forever. This is not a flash sale. It's a vault. It should feel like gaining access to an exclusive club.

  • Auction listing pages with 30-day countdown and live bid display
  • Starting bid of $1,200 — design around a premium price point
  • Post-win flow: size selection, frame selection, digital CoA delivery
  • State for auctions with no bids (remains open until sold)
  • Clear differentiation between collector's editions and gallery editions
  • Auction marketing strategy (email and social campaign concept)
Scarcity is the point. But, be careful! There's a difference between urgency and pressure.
04Gallery editions

Gallery editions are the always-available entry point. They are limited to 50 prints per image, purchasable directly, no auction required. The design challenge is communicating that gallery editions are genuinely valuable without making collector's editions feel inaccessible.

  • Browsable gallery experience with filtering
  • Print detail page with full size/frame selector and AR integration
  • Clear communication of the 50-print limit without pressure tactics
  • Visual and UX distinction from the auction system
Gallery editions are not a backup option. They are how most people will first own a Range & Focus print.
05Trail materials

A significant part of how Range & Focus connects with new customers happens on trail — literally. While hiking, the photographer encounters people who are curious about the work. Right now, he gives them a sticker. A sticker is a good start, but it doesn't tell people where to go. Your team should propose what comes with it.

  • Something that fits in a fully-loaded camera bag with weight and size as big constraints
  • Something that encourages a website visit, once the hiker has internet service
  • A business card is the obvious answer, but what's the unexpected one?
  • Whatever it is, it should feel unique to Range & Focus.
The bar: would you actually stop on a steep switchback trail to hand this to a stranger? If yes, you're on the right track.
06Social media

Range & Focus lives primarily on Instagram (we should probably be on TikToc too). The brand needs to grow its reels presence, not because reels are trending, but because moving images of still landscapes are genuinely interesting and reels help to grow new audiences. Ads and organic posts serve different purposes and should be designed differently.

  • Organic post designs (grid aesthetic, storytelling cadence, caption voice)
  • Paid ad concepts: what does a Range & Focus ad actually say?
  • Reels strategy: what content, what cadence, what format?
  • Auction announcement content: building anticipation for a 30-day window
  • Campaign concept for a gallery edition launch
Ads and posts are not the same thing. An ad asks for something. A post gives something. Both need to feel like the same brand but do distinctly different things.
07Sizzle reel

A 30–60 second reel that explains what Range & Focus is, why it's different, and makes someone want to buy a print. This deliverable is a storyboard concept and production plan, detailed enough that someone could pick it up and shoot from it.

  • Full storyboard (shot-by-shot, with timing and copy)
  • Shot list and location/setup notes
  • Audio concept: music direction, voiceover or no voiceover
  • Editing approach: how does it feel? What's the rhythm?
  • Proposed distribution: where does this live and how is it used?
Range & Focus does not use AI-generated imagery. The sizzle reel should honor that with real footage, real landscapes, and a real sense of craft.

Photography direction

Range & Focus photography is earned in the miles it takes to get them. The images are made in conditions most people don't even explore. We set out on a 10-mile trail at golden hour or before dawn breaks. We venture down rivers, across deserts, up mountans, and through canyouns. Metrics for success

Reaching the Summit: How we’ll know we’ve arrived?

There are six performance indicators for this project. Each team will be evaluated on all six. The winning concepts will be strong across all of them.

01

On-time delivery

All deliverables completed and presented by each milestone deadline. 100% of the work, on time, every time.

The client who waits for your deck is already thinking about your replacement.

02

Conceptual cohesion

Every deliverable — UX, brand, social, print, video — feels like it came from the same mind. No fragmented projects.

A great social post can't save a bad UX. They rise together or they sink together.

03

Feature integrity

The AR viewer, auction system, and gallery edition experience are fully thought through. Edge cases addressed. Flows complete.

What happens when someone wins an auction at 2am and their card declines? That screen matters too.

04

Quality of execution

Pixel-perfect, well-organized, and production-ready. Files that could be handed to a Shopify developer without a follow-up email.

A developer opening your Figma file should feel relief, not dread.

05

Strategic depth

Decisions are traceable back to research and brand strategy — not just aesthetic preference. The team can defend every call.

"It looked good" is not a strategy. "Our collector persona needs trust signals above the fold" is.

06

Creative risk

The concept does something the competition hasn't done, or does something familiar in a way that is distinctly Range & Focus.

Safe work wins nothing. The brief says "design like it's going live." Take that seriously.

Pro tips from the client

Straight from the trail.

  • Every image in the Range & Focus catalog was earned on foot — often miles from the nearest road, in light that lasts about thirty seconds. That is the story. Every design decision you make should reinforce that the work is genuine, hard-won, and irreplaceable. If the design starts to feel like stock photography or generic e-commerce, it has lost the thread.

  • There is a meaningful difference between urgency and pressure. Urgency is honest — five editions exist, and when they are gone they are gone. Pressure is manipulative — countdown timers that scream, language that guilt-trips, layouts that bully. Range & Focus is a brand built on trust. The scarcity should feel like a fact of the work, not a tactic of the store.

  • If a buyer has to hunt for the AR option, or if it feels like a bonus feature tacked on at the end of the purchase flow, it has not been designed well enough. The best version of this feature makes the customer feel like they are standing in their own living room with a print in their hands — before they have spent a dollar. Design the experience around that feeling, not around the technology.

  • The photographs are real because the locations are real and the miles were real. AI-generated imagery undermines that entirely. The sizzle reel deliverable must use real footage — no AI generation, no synthetic landscapes, no composited scenes that did not happen. This applies to every visual deliverable in the project. When in doubt, ask.

  • Weight and volume are real constraints on the trail. A folded brochure in a full camera bag is a liability. Whatever your team proposes for trail materials should be light, compact, and durable — and it should feel like it belongs in that environment, not like it was grabbed from a printer tray. Think about what you would actually carry on a switchback, and design from there.

  • This is not a hypothetical exercise. The best concept from this project will be evaluated for actual implementation on the Range & Focus Shopify build. That means your decisions carry real weight — your information architecture, your component design, your copy, your brand extensions. Design like it is going live, because it might.
